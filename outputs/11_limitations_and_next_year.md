# Survey limitations and suggestions for next year

## Limitations

### Who answered
1. **Self-selected festival visitors.** People who stop at a sustainability festival likely care more than the typical student or employee, so results describe engaged people, not the whole university.
2. **Unequal incentive.** Students earned a stamp toward a free water bottle; staff had no equivalent. This likely explains student over-representation (81% of sample vs. 77% of school; 87% at Campus B) and may encourage fast answers (28% of students ticked all four "high-impact" options).
3. **Staff are thin.** 63 staff in total, 21 at Campus B (13% of that sample vs. 23% of the school). Staff results have margins of about +/-12 points and cannot be split further.
4. **Possible repeat or shared responses.** There is no respondent ID, so the same person could have answered twice (four rows share a timestamp) or at both festivals.

### What was asked
5. **Two different surveys under one title.** Staff answered department questions; students answered program questions. No question was shared, so staff and students cannot be compared.
6. **No questions about actual behavior.** Students were asked what they know, believe and want; nothing about what they do. "Gaps" are therefore awareness and interest gaps, not true intention-to-action gaps.
7. **The "existing programs" question is ambiguous.** It is unclear whether every option is real; with no decoys, ticking a program cannot be told apart from guessing. We call it "recognized", not "aware".
8. **Check-all lists are weak at ranking.** Picking everything is easy, and "reduce" had only one option versus several recycling-type options.
9. **Staff were asked about their department.** 28 of 43 answers were "no", "unsure" or similar, which suggests many do not know what their department does. The question measures knowledge of the department as much as practice.
10. **Open text is thin.** Only three questions were open-ended (all staff) and most answers were one word, so word counts support themes, not rankings.
11. **Email collected in a free-text answer** mixes contact details with opinions and creates a privacy burden.

### How it was analyzed
12. **Campus is inferred from the date.** Campus differences are tangled up with day, weather and event layout; any difference could be a "day" difference.
13. **Many tests, small groups.** 23 campus comparisons were run; none held up after correction, and the regression found no predictors. "Not significant" means "could not tell", not "no difference".
14. **Text coding is rule-based.** Staff answers were sorted into "doing something / unsure" and "wants help" by simple rules; a different reader might sort a few differently.

## Suggestions for next year

### Design
- **Ask a shared core of questions of everyone** (role plus the same awareness, belief and interest items), then a short role-specific section. This allows student-vs-staff comparisons.
- **Add behavior questions.** For example: "In the last 30 days, how often did you bring a reusable bottle or cup?", "Have you donated, swapped or repaired something on campus?" Use the same scale for each.
- **Replace the single "which are programs" tick-list with a per-program ladder:** *never heard of it / heard of it / used it*. This gives an awareness-to-use funnel for every program, not just the Teams group. Add one or two made-up programs as decoys to measure guessing.
- **Ask whether a requested program exists** ("Do you think this already exists on campus?") so demand for the new can be told apart from demand for the known.
- **Limit check-all questions** ("pick your top two") or ask for a rating on each activity, and give "reduce" and "reuse" as many options as "recycle".
- **Rephrase the department question** to "As far as you know, is your department..." with a "don't know" option, and collect the answer in a closed list plus an optional comment.
- **Ask Yes/No questions with a middle option** ("maybe", "tell me later") so "yes to learn more" is less automatic.

### Data collection
- **Record the campus and the festival directly** instead of inferring it from the date, and add a short random code or a "have you already taken this survey?" question.
- **Collect emails in a separate form** linked only by consent.
- **Add basic grouping questions** (for example undergraduate/graduate, staff vs. faculty, department type) so subgroups can be compared without identifying anyone.
- **Make staff participation easier or rewarded** (same incentive, or send the survey by email to departments) with a goal of about 23% of respondents.
- **Set sample-size targets in advance.** About 100 responses per role per campus gives roughly +/-10 points per group; about 380 total gives +/-5 points overall.

### Analysis
- **Decide the main comparisons before collecting data** (for example "campus by role on program use") to limit chance findings.
- **Pre-test the survey** with 5-10 people and check how long it takes (a very short completion time can flag rushed answers).
- **Keep the same core questions in the same wording** so year-over-year change can be measured.
